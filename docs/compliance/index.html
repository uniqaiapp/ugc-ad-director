AI-generated UGC ad compliance checklist

What to disclose, what to avoid and what to prove in a UGC ad made with AI video. Every rule that lint enforces is shown below with a failing and a passing example.

What does an AI-generated UGC ad have to disclose?

An AI-generated UGC ad needs a plain on-screen label from the first frame, the platform's AI-content setting switched on, a fictional presenter who only demonstrates, and a dated source for every measurable claim. Put the label in a caption, not in a corner the app can cover. This is a working checklist, not legal advice.

Working checklist, not legal advice

These rules are a working checklist for ad drafts. Policies change, so read each platform's current ad policy before a campaign goes live. The dates in the source file are from 2026-10-28.

FTC rules for fake testimonials in AI UGC ads

As checked on 2026-10-28, and summarised from references/compliance.md: the FTC rule on fake reviews and testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465 (effective 21 October 2024), prohibits testimonials by people who do not exist or who did not use the product, including AI-generated ones.

  • The FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255 (revised 2023), require endorsements to reflect honest experience and material connections to be disclosed.
  • An AI presenter is a demonstrator. It can show, explain, compare and ask, and it cannot say “I’ve used this” or “not sponsored”. Lint fails those phrasings for AI and no-presenter scripts.
  • A real customer or creator can testify, with a signed release, proof of purchase and a paid-partnership disclosure in speech and on screen.

A working checklist, not legal advice. Read the rule text before you run a campaign.

1. Disclose AI-generated content

  • Put a plain label in an on-screen caption from the first frame: AI-generated ad, or AI-generated ad (b-roll) when only some shots are generated. Lint fails if the label is missing from every caption while any shot is generated.
  • Switch on the platform's AI-content setting when you upload (compliance.platform_toggle). TikTok, Meta and YouTube each have their own wording and scope, so read the live policy.
  • EU: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act), Article 50, sets transparency duties for AI-generated or manipulated content that resembles real people, places or events, with application dates from August 2026.
  • Do not hide the label in a corner the platform UI covers. A caption line is safest.

2. Never fabricate testimonials

  • An AI presenter is a demonstrator. It can show, explain, compare and ask. It cannot say "I've used this", "changed my life", "honest review" or "not sponsored". Lint fails these phrasings for AI and no-presenter scripts.
  • Star ratings, "thousands of happy customers", review screenshots and "verified buyer" wording are claims about real people. They need real sources and belong in claims with evidence, or stay out.
  • US: the FTC rule on fake reviews and testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465 (effective 21 October 2024), prohibits testimonials by people who do not exist or who did not use the product, including AI-generated ones. The Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255 (revised 2023), require honest experience and disclosure of material connections.
  • A real customer or creator can testify. Get a signed release, keep proof of purchase and disclose the paid relationship in speech and on screen.

3. Never impersonate people, creators or brands

  • presenter.description must describe a fictional person. Do not reference a real creator, actor, customer or employee, and do not use someone's photo as the start frame without written consent for this use.
  • No platform or third-party endorsement wording such as "as seen on TikTok", "Instagram approved" or "endorsed by". Lint fails these.
  • Keep logos, flags, carrier names, airline liveries and competitor packaging out of generated shots. The default negative prompt covers text, watermark and logos.

4. Claims need evidence

  • Anything that sounds measurable is a claim: "clinically", "proven", "guaranteed", "#1", "dermatologist-tested", percentages, "results in N days", "lose N". Each goes in claims with where it is proven. Lint fails unlisted ones and warns on unlisted numbers.
  • Health, weight, skin-condition, financial-return and "safe for" wording are regulated categories on every major ad platform. Prefer observable demos to outcome promises.
  • Prices and offers: say them only if they are true on the day and for the run of the ad. Otherwise write "link below".
  • Before and after: a split screen that shows application is a demo. A split screen that implies a result over time is a claim and needs a source.

What lint checks, rule by rule

Each failing example below was run through lint when this page was built. The message is what the checker shows for it.

The AI label is empty

Generated footage needs a label. An empty field means the ad would run without one.

✕ Fails compliance.ai_label

"ai_label": ""

Error: Add an on-screen AI label such as "AI-generated ad": the ad has generated footage or an AI presenter.

✓ Passes

"ai_label": "AI-generated ad"

The label is not in any caption

The label only counts when it is on screen in a caption, from the first frame.

✕ Fails compliance.ai_label

shot 1 caption: "Your foundation isn't the problem."

Error: The label "AI-generated ad" is not in any caption. Add it as a caption line from the first shot.

✓ Passes

shot 1 caption: "Your foundation isn't the problem.\nAI-generated ad"

The platform AI switch is not on

Set platform_toggle to true once the AI-content setting is switched on in the ad manager.

✕ Fails compliance.platform_toggle

"platform_toggle": false

Warning: Switch on the platform's AI-content setting when you upload, then tick the box for it here.

✓ Passes

"platform_toggle": true

The AI presenter has no description

A written description helps keep the presenter fictional, so it is not mistaken for a real person.

✕ Fails presenter.description

"description": ""

Warning: Describe the AI presenter in a sentence, as a fictional person who is not modelled on anyone real.

✓ Passes

"description": "fictional woman, late twenties, no makeup, towel on hair; generated, not based on anyone"

An AI presenter speaks as a customer

With an AI or no presenter, first-person experience wording is a fabricated testimonial. Lint fails it.

✕ Fails shot 2 voiceover

voiceover: "I've been using this serum for a month and my skin is glowing."

Error: “I've been using” reads as a customer testimonial, and an AI presenter cannot give one. Rewrite it as a demo or a sourced claim.

✓ Passes

voiceover: "Dry skin drinks the water out of your makeup, so it cakes by lunch."

The caption implies an endorsement

Wording such as "as seen on" or "endorsed by" suggests that a platform or third party backs the ad.

✕ Fails shot 6 caption

caption: "as seen on TikTok. link below"

Error: “as seen on” suggests a platform or another party endorses the ad. Remove it.

✓ Passes

caption: "link below. try it under your usual base"

A claim is not in the claims list

Wording like "best-selling" is a claim. It has to be listed with the place where it is proven.

✕ Fails shot 6 caption

caption: "best-selling serum. link below"

Error: "best-selling" is a claim. Add it to the claims list with evidence, or take it out.

✓ Passes

claims: "best-selling serum" with an evidence entry

A number has no claim behind it

Numbers in a caption or voiceover need an entry in claims. Without one, lint warns.

✕ Fails shot 6 caption

caption: "over 4000 jars sold. link below"

Warning: The number "4000" has no source. Add it to the claims list with evidence, or take it out.

✓ Passes

claims: "over 4000 jars sold" with an evidence entry

The hook is too long

A hook over 12 words does not fit in 2 seconds.

✕ Fails hook.text

hook: "Your foundation is not the problem, and here is what it really is, trust me"

Error: The hook is 15 words. Keep it to 12 words or fewer so it lands inside 2 seconds.

✓ Passes

hook: "Your foundation isn't the problem."

The hook shot ends after 2 seconds

The first shot must end by 2 s.

✕ Fails shot 1 end_s

"end_s": 3

Error: The hook shot ends at 3s. End it by 2s so the hook lands in time.

✓ Passes

"end_s": 2

A caption line is too long

A caption line is capped at 48 characters, so it fits a phone screen.

✕ Fails shot 3 caption

caption: "two drops of serum, pressed in with two fingers, never rubbed"

Warning: This caption line is 61 characters. Keep each line to 48 characters max so it fits a phone screen.

✓ Passes

caption: "two drops, press, don't rub"

The voiceover is too fast

Above 3.5 words per second a shot is hard to follow. Lint warns.

✕ Fails shot 2 voiceover

voiceover: 27 words in a 5 s shot

Warning: The voiceover speaks 5.4 words per second over 5s, above the 3.5 words per second pace. Cut words or give the shot more time.

✓ Passes

voiceover: 14 words in a 5 s shot

Pre-flight checklist

  • python3 scripts/ugc_script.py lint script.json --strict passes
  • AI label visible in the first two seconds, not cropped by the platform UI
  • Platform AI-content setting switched on at upload
  • Presenter is fictional, or a real person with release and disclosure
  • Every entry in claims has a document behind it, dated
  • No real logos, people or competitor products in generated shots
  • Offer and price in the CTA are true for the run dates
  • Policy pages of each target platform re-read this quarter
